How one lesson works

Print the lesson. Use the guide. Let the drawing happen.

The Field Kit is built around a simple back-and-forth rhythm: the student works on a clean page, and the parent uses the matching support page when guidance is needed.

1

Print the student page

Your student draws directly on the worksheet. No app, video, or separate sketchbook setup.

2

Check the support guide

The guide mirrors the worksheet with examples, prompts, and technique notes for the parent.

3

Return to the drawing

Use the guide only when it helps, then let the student keep working independently.

What to watch for

Real progress should show up in ordinary lesson moments.

Torch & Trowel is still gathering family stories. For now, these are the specific results the curriculum is designed to make easier to notice after one lesson.

Parent who does not draw

The support guide lowers the pressure to perform.

Instead of inventing an explanation, the parent can point to examples, prompts, and reminders already built into the guide.

Student who rushes

The worksheet gives one focused place to slow down.

Short boxes, repeated exercises, and pencil control work make the goal smaller than “make a perfect drawing.”

Screen-weary school day

The lesson can happen with paper, pencil, and attention.

No video queue, login, app, tablet, or online classroom has to become part of the art lesson.
