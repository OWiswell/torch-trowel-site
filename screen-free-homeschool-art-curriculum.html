Unit 01 / Lines in Control

Why the first lesson starts with lines.

Before a student can draw a convincing object, they need to feel what a pencil can do. Lines in Control starts there: simple marks, clear practice, and visible progress.

Matt Tolbert July 7, 2026 5 min read

It can feel almost too simple to begin a drawing curriculum with lines. But line is where the student first meets the pencil. A line can be light or heavy, straight or curved, smooth or shaky, rushed or intentional.

That is why Drawing Field Kit Vol. 1 begins with Lines in Control. The goal is not to make a finished artwork right away. The goal is to help the student notice that their hand can make choices on purpose.

Control comes before complexity.

Beginners often want to jump straight into people, animals, buildings, or scenes. Those are good goals, but they ask for many skills at once. A student has to control pressure, direction, spacing, proportion, and confidence before the object even looks like itself.

Lines in Control lowers the number of problems. The student practices straight, curved, and tapered lines. They repeat marks. They compare line quality. They begin to see that drawing is not magic; it is a set of choices they can practice.

Line variety gives students a vocabulary.

A student who only has one kind of line has one kind of answer. When they learn line variety, they gain options. A light line can search. A darker line can clarify. A repeated line can create texture. A curved line can show motion or softness. A controlled line can make the page feel less random.

The first lesson is not just about making lines. It is about helping the student realize that marks can be intentional.

Visible practice builds confidence.

Line practice gives students evidence they can see. A second row may be steadier than the first. A repeated texture may become more consistent. A tapered line may begin awkwardly and then become smoother.

That visible improvement matters. Confidence does not have to arrive before the work. Sometimes it comes after the student sees one mark become more controlled.

Parent note

In Unit 01, watch for intention more than perfection. Ask, “Can you make that line slower?” or “Can you make three versions of that mark?”

Why this belongs at the beginning.

Lines in Control gives the rest of the Field Kit something to build on. Empty space, simple shapes, forms, and real objects all depend on the student’s ability to make marks with attention. Starting with lines is not a detour from drawing. It is the doorway into it.
